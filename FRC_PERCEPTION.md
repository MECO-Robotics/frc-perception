# FRC Perception: REBUILT 2026

## Goal

Extend the existing Scout Edge Vision project into a robot-facing perception service for the 2026 FIRST Robotics Competition game, REBUILT. Use the already configured and calibrated stereo camera to detect FUEL, estimate each visible piece's position, group nearby pieces into clusters, and publish timestamped results over NetworkTables.

REBUILT uses one game-piece type, FUEL. The detector must be trained or adapted for the actual FUEL appearance; the current COCO model is useful for plumbing and video tests, but is not a FUEL detector. See [FIRST's 2026 game materials](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/season-materials).

## Existing Scout capabilities to reuse

- Stereo capture splits the camera into synchronized left and right images.
- `StereoDepth` loads `media/calibration/stereo_calibration.yml` and estimates disparity and metric depth for detection boxes.
- The pipeline maintains a bounded, latest-frame inference queue and has a live video/debug UI.
- Detector results already include image boxes, confidence, and optional depth.

## FUEL model candidate and smoke test

The selected first candidate is [FRC 9550's YOLO26n model](https://huggingface.co/frc9550/2026-game-piece), published under Apache-2.0. The model card describes one `ball` class and training on about 4,500 team photos plus synthetic images. The downloaded checkpoint is `models/rebuilt_fuel_yolo26n.pt` (SHA-256 `6de918b938c5e0603eb49742b3c2ad523aa0e0ab7907d4918366e736d5f870eb`).

A CPU smoke test loaded the checkpoint with Ultralytics 8.4.157 and ran it at 640 pixels on Project516's public sample image, which contains one large FUEL. At confidence 0.25 it returned one `ball` detection at 0.854 confidence. This confirms the weights load and produce a plausible detection; it is not an independent accuracy evaluation.

The live Scout camera is connected at 3200x1200 side-by-side MJPG, with 1600x1200 per eye. A 640x640 FP16 TensorRT engine was built and loaded by the service. On the current dim indoor view, two partly occluded, ball-like pieces are visible behind a monitor. Full-frame CPU inference at 640 and 960 returned zero boxes, and the running TensorRT service returned zero boxes at confidence 0.30. Raising camera gain from 120 to 255 did not produce detections; gain was restored to 120. Focused 90x90 crops around the two visible pieces produced TensorRT `ball` candidates at 0.46 and 0.61 confidence. The image is too small and occluded to establish these as official FUEL detections.

A fresh capture confirmed the same view. Lowering the running service threshold to 0.10 still returned no boxes. Centered 128x128 TensorRT crops produced weaker candidates at 0.14 for the white/orange piece and 0.26 for the yellow piece; larger and combined crops often returned no boxes. A warm-color connected-component proposal pass merged the two nearby pieces and also triggered high-confidence crop detections on unrelated warm objects elsewhere in the image, so it is not suitable as-is. These crop detections are not yet integrated into the live full-frame pipeline.

Additional proposal tests did not generalize. An overlapping 128x128 scan over the full left image produced 322 crop detections, mostly on background; it returned no box near the yellow piece and only tiny boxes near the white/orange one. A Hough-circle plus warm-color proposal pass found hundreds of room candidates and its model-filtered detections were on decor. Project516's alternate YOLO26 v1 and v0 checkpoints also failed to produce two correct full-frame boxes on this view. The current target visibility is insufficient to distinguish the two expected FUEL pieces from occluded room objects reliably.

The active stereo calibration was recomputed from the saved AndyMark 15x11 ChArUco pairs using the board settings shown in the UI (15 mm squares, 10 mm markers). It accepted 16 pairs with 0.79 px stereo RMS and a 63.03 mm baseline, close to the configured 63.5 mm. The prior calibration is backed up at `.backups/stereo_calibration_before_15mm_board.yml`. Scout restarted and reports the new focal length. This improves the calibration fit, but no known-distance target has validated range accuracy.

Distance remains unverified. On the focused crop boxes, the current rectified matcher reports about 0.58 m and 0.48 m with the runtime's 1.466 disparity multiplier; using raw calibrated disparity (multiplier 1.0) changes those to about 0.84 m and 0.71 m. Raw-image patch triangulation is near 1.0 m for both. The [2026 game manual](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/HTML/2026GameManual.htm) specifies a 15 cm FUEL diameter; the crop-box apparent size suggests roughly 2 m, but the objects are occluded and the boxes are crop-derived. These methods disagree, so none of the values is field-validated. A zoomed crop-inference diagnostic image is `runs/detect/frc-live-two-piece-diagnostic.jpg`.

The Scout decoder already supports YOLO26's end-to-end six-column output. The FUEL engine now supplies the `ball` class name, and the running service is configured for the FUEL model with rectified stereo and inference enabled without a video viewer. A successful service start and a working `/api/detections` endpoint confirm the runtime path; useful detection and range output still need better visibility/data and validation.

## First milestone

Build a robot-facing path that can be exercised with recorded video before connecting to the robot:

1. Add a FUEL detector interface/profile, keeping model choice replaceable. Start with a labeled dataset of real REBUILT FUEL under field lighting, viewpoints, occlusion, and motion blur; do not interpret COCO detections as FUEL.
2. Convert each detection plus stereo depth into a camera-relative 3D point using the calibrated intrinsics and the detection's image location. Reject invalid disparity/depth and report confidence/quality instead of inventing coordinates.
3. Group points into spatial clusters with a configurable distance threshold. Publish both individual pieces and clusters so robot code can choose its own behavior.
4. Publish compact, versioned NetworkTables topics with timestamps, frame sequence, camera/calibration state, and validity. Keep camera-relative coordinates explicit until a measured camera-to-robot transform is configured.
5. Add a local replay/debug mode that overlays IDs, depth, and cluster membership and reports detection and publish rates.

## NetworkTables contract (proposed v1)

Use a configurable team number and server address. On the roboRIO network, connect as a client to the configured NT4 server. Publish under `/FRC/Perception/v1`:

- `status` (string): `starting`, `ready`, `degraded`, or `error`.
- `frame` (int): monotonically increasing frame sequence.
- `timestamp` (double): capture/pipeline timestamp in seconds; document its clock domain.
- `timestampClock` (string): `gstreamer_pts_seconds`; this is the camera stream PTS, not roboRIO FPGA time.
- `pieces` (JSON string): array of `{id, x_m, y_m, z_m, confidence, depth_quality, track_state, position_age_ms, range_age_ms}`. Coordinates are null if the last stereo range is older than the configured freshness limit.
- `clusters` (JSON string): array of `{id, x_m, y_m, z_m, count, confidence, piece_ids}`, clustered in 3D with `FRC_CLUSTER_RADIUS_M` (default 0.25 m).
- `calibrated` (boolean): whether metric stereo geometry is available.

Coordinate convention for v1 should be camera-relative: +X right in the image, +Y down, +Z forward from the camera. A later robot-frame transform must declare its axes and be independently measured. Do not use the existing calibration's raw camera baseline as a mount transform.

Prefer typed NT topics for scalar status and metadata. JSON arrays keep variable-length piece/cluster snapshots atomic and easy to inspect during the first integration; if bandwidth or robot parsing becomes an issue, move the arrays to typed struct or packed array topics while versioning the contract.

## Calibration and geometry checks

The project has `media/calibration/stereo_calibration.yml` and a separately named `stereo_calibration_unverified.yml`. Confirm which calibration was measured on the deployed Scout camera and verify its image dimensions, baseline, rectification state, and known-distance error before treating results as field-ready. The current runtime can calculate box distance, but a ground-plane location is not justified unless camera pitch/height and the target contact point are modeled. Until then, publish camera-relative 3D points with stated quality and avoid claiming field coordinates.

## Configuration to add

Use `FRC_`-prefixed settings, separate from the current general Scout demo defaults:

- `FRC_NT_MODE` (`client` or `server`), `FRC_NT_SERVER`, `FRC_TEAM_NUMBER`, and `FRC_NT_TABLE`.
- `FRC_GAMEPIECE_MODEL`, confidence threshold, clustering radius, maximum publish rate, and stale-data timeout.
- `FRC_CAMERA_TO_ROBOT_TRANSFORM` only after the mount transform has been measured.

Do not commit team-specific addresses or credentials. NetworkTables normally uses the robot's existing NT4 server and team configuration rather than a new credential.

## Delivery sequence

1. Confirm the runtime and camera/calibration on Scout; record measured camera mode and calibration provenance.
2. Implement FUEL detection and a replay evaluation set with hand-reviewed labels.
3. Implement per-piece 3D projection and spatial clustering; validate coordinates against measured targets at several distances.
4. Add the NT4 publisher and contract/version metadata; test with a desktop NT client or robot simulator.
5. Integrate a measured camera-to-robot transform and robot-side consumer, then validate on the robot and field.

### NT4 package (2026-11-03)

Added an optional NT4 publisher in `scout/nt4.py` and wired it to each flow-frame snapshot. It publishes typed status/frame/timestamp/calibration topics and atomic JSON snapshots for pieces and 3D clusters. NT disconnects report `degraded`; empty or stale stereo range yields pieces with null XYZ and no clusters, avoiding invented range. Coordinates are camera-relative after lens undistortion/rectification; no camera-to-robot mount transform is applied. The service unit and environment template are in `deploy/frc-perception.service` and `deploy/frc-perception.env.example`.

The Jetson deployment uses Python 3.8 on Ubuntu 20.04/glibc 2.31, for which RobotPy's current PyPI ARM64 wheels are not compatible. `scripts/install-frc-nt-jetson.sh` installs RobotPy's Python 3.8 ARM64 NTCore 2023.4.3 wheel and its matching native dependencies from the RobotPy ARM wheel index. The NT4 client API is present in that package. A recent RobotPy/wpi build should replace this pinned compatibility package when the Scout base OS/Python is upgraded. Team 8324 is used in the local environment template; change `FRC_TEAM_NUMBER` or set `FRC_NT_SERVER` for a different server.

The `frc-perception` user unit is installed but intentionally left disabled until explicitly started. The previous `scout-edge` unit was stopped after packaging. No live camera restart or NT robot connection was performed during this packaging step.

## Acceptance criteria

- Known-distance stereo estimates meet an agreed error bound over the intended collection range.
- Labeled replay evaluation reports precision/recall for FUEL at a documented confidence threshold.
- Cluster positions are stable enough for the robot use case and include count, confidence, timestamp, and freshness semantics.
- NT disconnects and camera/detector failures set a non-ready status and stop stale data from being mistaken for current observations.
- The robot consumer and publisher agree on version, coordinate frame, units, and timestamp meaning.

### Stereo and sphere-size checks

Before selecting the high-resolution engine, the live 640 service was sampled for 62 unique frames at confidence 0.10. The left ball was detected in 62/62 frames (median range 0.31 m, range 0.20-0.73 m, median absolute deviation 0.10 m); the right ball was detected in 60/62 (median 1.06 m, range 0.79-2.29 m, MAD 0.21 m). The selected live 1280/0.30 config was then validated for 102 unique frames: both balls appeared in 102/102, with zero extra boxes and median confidence 0.85/0.81. Its stereo medians remained 0.31 m and 0.98 m (MAD 0.09 m and 0.16 m), so the improved detector did not fix depth.

A later 127-frame live sample of 1280/0.30 also detected both targets in every frame with no extra boxes. Raw stereo depth medians were 0.31 m and 0.97 m, with MAD 0.09 m and 0.14 m; the second ball's range spanned 0.21-2.60 m. Trailing temporal medians reduced MAD to 0.01/0.13 m at 3 frames, 0.01/0.10 m at 5, and 0.00/0.09 m at 7, without count loss in complete windows. At the measured 133 ms frame interval, these windows add about 133/265/398 ms of age. A 3-frame median therefore exceeds the robot consumer's 150 ms freshness limit once Scout's capture-to-publish latency is included. Temporal filtering alone is not a suitable fix for robot-facing depth.

### Cluster-radius sweep

Using the seven frames with both detections and Hough-derived sphere centers, the estimated center-to-center distance was 0.136 m median (0.131-0.172 m). A 0.12 m radius kept two separate clusters in 7/7 frames; 0.14 m did so in 2/7; the robot project's current 0.15 m `clusterRadius` did so in only 1/7. The current radius would merge these adjacent balls in most sampled frames. At the time, 0.12 m appeared to be a candidate when each FUEL must remain separate. That sweep used monocular size-based positions and is superseded by the live stereo-order experiment below; it had not been checked against a measured distance.

On seven paired frames with both detections, default rectified SGBM (128 disparities, block size 5, uniqueness 8) produced median unscaled ranges of 0.60 m and 1.63 m. Varying block size and uniqueness did not resolve the inter-ball mismatch. Expanding to 192 disparities made the two estimates look consistent (0.23 m and 0.29 m), but those ranges conflict with the balls' apparent size and are likely incorrect matches. Left-right consistency filtering retained the same disparity medians (160.7 px and 46.8 px) with different valid-pixel support, so it did not correct the first ball's result.

The FRC 9550 boxes at 1280 had median apparent diameters of 99.2 px and 112.0 px. A Hough-circle refinement within each detector box found circles in all seven frames for both balls, with median radii 51.8 px and 54.6 px (median absolute deviations 0.4 px and 1.2 px). Using the nominal 0.15 m FUEL diameter and 1054 px focal length gives size-based ranges of about 1.53 m and 1.45 m. This is a much better same-plane consistency check than current SGBM, but it remains an unvalidated monocular estimate until compared with the user's tape measurement.

Using those Hough circles as masks for SGBM did not solve stereo. At 50% radius, the first ball had enough valid disparity pixels in only 1/7 frames; at 70%, it reached 6/7 but the median ranges were 0.83 m and 1.34 m; at 90%, support reached 7/7 but ranges diverged to 0.36 m and 1.50 m. The interior has too little reliable stereo texture, while including more of the edge reintroduces mixed/background pixels. Earlier stereo tests remained unreliable with the saved camera order; see the live camera-order experiment below.

The live candidate was then sampled over 60 unique frames at YOLO26n 1280 / confidence 0.30: both balls were detected in 60/60 frames with no extra boxes. ### Live detector-seeded segmentation pilot

The available FRC 9550 and Project516 FUEL checkpoints are YOLO detection models (`task=detect`), not instance-segmentation models. As a quick refinement experiment, box-seeded GrabCut was run on 12 sequential live preview frames with both FUEL balls visible side by side. The detector returned both balls in 12/12 frames. GrabCut produced a mask for each detector box in 12/12, but mask area varied from about 7% to 90% of the box area; the smallest masks were fragmented seed-sized regions, and mask overlap varied from 0 to 237 pixels as the boxes overlapped. No mask labels were available for pixel-level accuracy scoring.

Across those 12 frames, detector-box center standard deviation was 0.48/0.48 px and 0.38/0.00 px (x/y) for balls 1/2. GrabCut centroid standard deviation was 0.24/0.05 px for ball 1, but 0.69/0.99 px for ball 2. Thus the color/shape segmentation refinement helped the first center slightly and made the second less stable on this static view. Keep detector boxes as the source for count and range. A trained instance-segmentation checkpoint and labeled mask replay would be needed before considering a segmentation model as a replacement.

I called the FP16 TensorRT detector directly on 60 evenly spaced left-camera frames from each saved clip, using confidence 0.05, NMS 0.15, and the same box-shape filter. Timing includes CPU preprocessing, H2D, TensorRT, D2H, and postprocessing, but excludes decode and stereo. The 960 engine at 0.02 has stronger 30-frame count evidence elsewhere, but this timing/count sweep used 0.05 consistently.

TensorRT `trtexec` with CUDA graph replay on the 640 engine reached 146 qps and 5.35 ms median GPU+transfer latency, but had a 5.93 ms p95 and a 51.5 ms p99 outlier; compute-time coefficient of variation was 122%. ### Kalman instance tracking across detector gaps (2026-11-03)

Added a constant-velocity Kalman tracker after stereo validation and the apparent-size gate, then extended it with per-frame sparse Lucas-Kanade flow. It associates boxes by class, predicted center, overlap, and compatible stereo range, then assigns stable per-run `track_id` values. `/api/detections` and WebRTC expose `tracks` with measured, flow, or predicted state, track age, visual age, confidence decay, and range age. The regular `boxes` list contains fresh detector measurements only. A track may be reported for up to 500 ms after its last detector anchor, but after 120 ms without a visual flow update it is omitted. These limits are configurable with `SCOUT_TRACK_MAX_AGE_MS`, `SCOUT_TRACK_FLOW_GRACE_MS`, and `SCOUT_TRACK_DECAY_MS`; source changes reset IDs/state.

### Hybrid tracker replay validation (2026-11-03)

I replayed both saved 60 fps clips through the production `FuelTracker`, advancing optical flow on each decoded left-camera frame and using cached confidence-0.05 1280 TensorRT detections every 3 frames (20 Hz) and every 7 frames (8.6 Hz).
